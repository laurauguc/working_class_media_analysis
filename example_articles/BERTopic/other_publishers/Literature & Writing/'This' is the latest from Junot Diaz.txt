Pulitzer Prize winner Junot Díaz's new story collection, This Is How You Lose Her, arrives Tuesday. Díaz, 43, who splits his time between Manhattan and Boston, spoke with USA TODAY's Deirdre Donahue.
Q: Yunior is a central character in your writing. Like you, he was born in the Dominican Republic, raised in working-class New Jersey  and attended Rutgers. Yunior is even the name your family calls you! Are you just ripping pages out of your diary?
A: I wish! It would be far easier. I wouldn't have to do any work.   You can give characters your vida, but they are certainly not you.
Q: You teach creative writing at MIT. Isn't that like teaching astrophysics at Juilliard?
A: It's more like teaching cooking in a state penitentiary. The students are on a very strict regimen that doesn't always encourage them to think about the arts as immediately pertinent to their lives.
Q: In the September Vogue, you were photographed  at Edith Wharton's estate. When you were growing up, did you ever think you'd be in a fashion magazine wearing a bowler hat?
A: Sister, when I was growing up in New Jersey, I didn't think I would be in any form of magazine. And certainly not hanging out with Annie Leibovitz and playing dress-up in Edith Wharton's old mansion.
