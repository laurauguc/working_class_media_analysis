Those who wish to give book gifts with a regional slant will have no trouble this year.  There's a vast array of quality fiction by local authors, ranging from the talented Susan Welch's first collection of short stories to public-radio star Garrison Keillor's latest novel, which skewers public radio!
 Fiction
 Here's a rundown in alphabetical order, by title:
 - Bitter Grounds (Hyperion, $ 22.95) is Edina author Sandra Benitez's second novel. It's more complex than her first, "A Place Where the Sea Remembers," which won a Minnesota Book Award; it follows three generations of a Salvadoran family.
- National reviews of Crowning the Queen of Love by Susan Welch (Coffee House, $ 13.95) have been universally favorable. Welch's collection of short stories treats the topic of love with deft realism.
 - The Dean's List (Ballantine, $ 24) is one of Jon Hassler's most emotionally satisfying books, as he returns to Rookery State 25 years after Leland and his pals played jazz in Hassler's "Rookery Blues."
 - Flesh Wounds (Nan Talese/ Doubleday, $ 21.95) is St. Paul native Mick Cochrane's first novel. Judging from the reviews, he's an old hand at portraying the dynamics of a family, this one suffering from sexual misconduct.
 - Floating Kingdom by George Rabasa (Coffee House, $ 21.95) is a stylish book about a family who guide illegal aliens across the border from Mexico to the United States.
 - A long way away in America's heartland, Idle Curiosity by Martha Bergland (Graywolf, $ 22.95) realistically and sympathetically portrays an Illinois farm family in crisis.
 - The title character in Lake Wobegon Boy by Garrison Keillor (Viking, $ 24.95) is a public-radio station manager who heads for the bright lights of New York City.
 - Living at Night ($ 10.95) by Mariana Romo-Carmona comes from Duluth's feminist-lesbian publisher Spinsters Ink, and deals with the coming out of a working-class Puerto Rican girl.
 - The Natural Father by Robert Lacy (New Rivers, $ 14.95) is a first collection by a regular Star Tribune reviewer. It's a finely wrought collection of stories of gritty life and moral uncertainties.
 - Another Star Tribune reviewer is romance writer Kathleen Eagle, who emerged in a breakout novel this year, The Night Remembers (Avon, $ 16), a beautifully crafted novel set in south Minneapolis.
 - New York poet Alfred Corn took a chance on Minneapolis' Mid-List Press to publish his first novel, Part of His Story ($ 24), a beautiful love story about a dramatist recovering from the loss of a partner to AIDS.
 - Your Oasis on Flame Lake by Lorna Landvik (Ballantine, $ 23) finds the Minneapolis author of "Patty Jane's House of Curl" back with a charming novel in ear-perfect dialogue about two Minnesota families.
 Non-fiction
 - Cleaning Up, by Minneapolis lawyer and former University of Minnesota Board of Regents head David Lebedoff (The Free Press, $ 25), reads like a novel but tells the true story of the Exxon Valdez oil spill and the subsequent trial, which resulted in a multimillion-dollar settlement.
 - England for All Seasons (Ballantine, $ 19.95) is Susan Allen Toth's latest informative and very personal travel book about her favorite country.
 - Macalester College anthropologist Jack Weatherford is out with yet another book of the anthro-techno variety, The History of Money (Crown, $ 25).
 - The Most Wonderful Books, edited by by Emily Buchwald and the late Michael Dorris (Milkweed, $ 14.95 paper), is a collection of reminiscences by famous authors about the first book that struck them to the bone.
 - Just off the presses in London and New Haven, and already reviewed enthusiastically by the Times of London, is Minnesota native Wheelock Whitney III's new book, Gericault in Italy (Yale University Press, $ 65, illustrated), a meticulous study of the travels of French artist Theodore Gericault in Italy in the early 19th century.
 - Pledging Allegiance, by Minneapolis marketing executive Lawrence Samuel (Smithsonian, $ 27.50), tells a fascinating story of how the government managed to sell a bad financial deal - U.S. Savings Bonds - to a patriotic citizenry during World War II.
 - Also for history buffs is John Quincy Adams (Knopf, $ 30) by University of Minnesota professor Paul C. Nagel, who gives an in-depth look at the second Adams family presidency.  The book has been widely reviewed - including a recent positive notice by presidential know-it-all Gore Vidal in the New Yorker.
 - Afton Historical Society Press keeps turning out luscious-looking coffee-table books, and the new one is no exception: We Hold This Treasure ($ 49) is the story of Gillette Children's Hospital in St. Paul..
 - Outdoor writer Sigurd E. Olson (1899-1982) turns out to be a complex character indeed in David Backes' A Wilderness Within (Minnesota, $ 24.95)
 - Everyone can use a collection like the new one edited and selected by University of Minnesota professor Jack Zipes and his collaborator, Sander Gilman: Yale Companion to Jewish Writing and Thought in German Culture, 1096-1996 (Yale, $ 45).
 In print as usual
 We'll close with four books by writers who are well-known to Minnesota newspaper readers.
 - Sid! by Star Tribune columnist Sid Hartman with Patrick Reusse (Voyageur, $ 24) unpacks lots of ideas by one of the country's most opinionated sports writers.
 - Minstrel: My Adventures in Newspapering is the autobiography of Jim Klobuchar (Minnesota, $ 17.95), who recently retired after years of entertaining and informing first Minneapolis Star, then Star Tribune, readers.
 - One of his old-time competitors was Robert T Smith, whose reminiscences of Newspaper Row 50 years ago and his subsequent career as a Time magazine writer are included in Robert T Smith Revisited (Nodin Press, $ 13). Smith, who did come home again, later wrote columns for the Tribune, many of which appear in this new book.
 - Also published by Nodin is Views from the Publisher's Desk ($ 25), by businessman, journalist and former governor Elmer L. Andersen.
