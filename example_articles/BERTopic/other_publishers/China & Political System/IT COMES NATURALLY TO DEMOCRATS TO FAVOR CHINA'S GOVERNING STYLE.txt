In his Aug. 15 column "Stand by Taiwan," Jack Kelly ventured that the Democrats were too beholden to Chinese campaign money to stand up for democracy in Taiwan. That view seems naively in error.
Consider what the Chinese government stands for.
It proclaims itself a defender of the working class against the evil capitalists and grubbing merchants. (However, in practice, it reliably chooses to conduct as much economic activity as possible through large, state-dominated firms favoring the well-connected.)
Proclaiming itself a lover of country, it favors globalist free-trade policies - policies that exploit the workers for the sake of foreign customers (and merchants). It distrusts religion and religious movements and takes every available means to diminish their influence in society.
Believing that words are important, the government devotes a disproportionate amount of attention to propaganda and "spin." Yet in matters of both inter nal rebellion and external offenders, that same government is quick to resort to firepower to get its way.
Distrustful of the average individual's wisdom and/or morals, the Chinese think it better that federal authority make as many of society's decisions as possible: Power is better vested in the hands of a small "enlightened" ruling clique who share the "proper" perspectives and values.
You do not have to pay someone to pursue his heart's desire. And Kelly errs in assuming the Chinese must pay liberal Democrats to favor their type of government. FRED ANDERSON
Ross
