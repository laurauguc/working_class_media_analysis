Argentina's economy, already crippled by nearly four years of recession, ground to a halt yesterday as the deeply unpopular government of President Fernando de la Rua faced its eighth general strike in two years.
The union-led strike against government spending cuts, curbs on bank withdrawals, and rising unemployment closed offices, factories and public transport across the country.
Demonstrators clashed with police in several cities and chanted slogans outside the home of powerful Economy Minister Domingo Cavallo in the capital.
Meanwhile, de la Rua met with his predecessor, Carlos Menem of the opposition Peronist Party, to seek joint policies to save Argentina from economic collapse. But their talks, which focused on further cuts in public spending and the possibility of Argentina's adopting the U.S. dollar as its currency, did little to appease the strikers.
Angered by spiraling unemployment and plunging living standards, tens of thousands of union workers stayed off the job.
Adding fuel to the fire, the Economy Ministry announced yesterday that the unemployment rate had risen to more than 18 percent in October, up from 15 percent a year earlier.
In Neuquen, 640 miles southwest of Buenos Aires, hundreds of state workers clashed with riot police amid flying rocks and tear gas. There was no immediate report of injuries.
Defying heavy rain, about 70 protesters crowded the entrance to Cavallo's home in Buenos Aires. They waved a huge banner with the face of working-class heroine Evita Peron.
"Look what these economic measures have done," said one demonstrator, Roberto Pietronavi, 43, pointing to a group of protesters' children eating a watery corn stew out of plastic bowls under the banner.
