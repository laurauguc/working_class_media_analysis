BODY:
Hard to believe it's already been a quarter-century since we entertained the notion that Y2K was going to shut down civilization.
Here we are, moving on to 2025, but before we do that, let's reflect on the ups and downs of Pittsburgh music in '24.
This isn't about Bruce Springsteen or Zach Bryan coming to town. This is a look at the local bands, venues and festivals that made news, and there was quite a bit of it.
Let's begin with the down.
Sudden Little fail
In April, there was much ado about Pittsburgh getting its own little Bonnaroo. It would be held in September in the Hazelwood Green, an idyllic, 40-acre site along the Mon that's in the process of development.
The Sudden Little Thrills Music Festival was set for two days with SZA, The Killers, St. Vincent, Wiz Khalifa and more. Pretty good bill.
"How are they putting a festival on an undeveloped plot with no parking and a two-lane road in and out?" smart people wondered.
C3 Presents - the Live Nation-owned, Austin, Texas-based management company that runs the Austin City Limits Music Festival, Lollapalooza and the Voodoo Music + Arts Experience - was confident that it would work.
The only reason we received for the sudden cancellation a month later was that it was "due to circumstances beyond our control."
Speculation was that the circumstance was the $255 ticket price in an economy where groceries were up about 20%.
WonderNotWorking
It wasn't just Sudden Little Thrills. There was no WonderWorks festival, which had debuted in May 2023 at Hartwood Acres with Hozier and Jason Mraz.
Presented by the Ohio-based Elevation Group, WonderWorks was a spinoff of the 2022 Maple House Festival, a one-off partnership with Ghost Hounds founder Thomas Tull, with headliners Jason Isbell and Black Pumas.
There was a presale for a second WonderWorks fest in May 2024, but a lineup was never announced and the festival quietly disappeared.
Four Chord furnace
Rishi Bahl, frontman for Eternal Boy, did not back away from the challenge of mounting a festival. He upped the ante for the 10th Four Chord Music Festival by bringing it back into the city after two summers at Wild Things Park.
He was also dealing with a site, Carrie Furnace in Rankin, with very little infrastructure or in-and-out access, and that would have been fine, possibly, if not for the 95-degree heat wave, lack of shade and a Sunday storm.
Everyone survived, thank goodness, and the moshpits were on for A Day to Remember, Senses Fail, etc., but fans were a little too hot and bothered, especially by the exhausting wait for a shuttle back to the Waterfront.
Expect a new location for Four Chord 11.
More weather news!
For the first time in what seems like ever, rain was not a major factor at either the Millvale Music Festival or the Northside Music Festival (formerly Deutschtown). They went off with no power outages, no blown-over tents and no radical shifting of schedules.
Millvale was super hot and crowded - attendees really want Grant Avenue closed to traffic - and yet successful and fun.
"We definitely had our largest festival and turnout to date - an estimated 20,000 people over the two days," says festival chair Paul Bossung.
Northside partied on just blocks away from Allegheny General Hospital, where people injured in the Trump assassination attempt in Butler were being transported.
"We avoided the rain this year but not the heat," notes director Ben Soltesz. "The average high for the July 13th weekend is 83 degrees, but we clocked 11 degrees above that, which made for a very hot few days."
They had a "tremendous" Friday night turnout for Bastard Bearded Irishmen and F--- Yeah Dinosaurs.
Coming in '26
When Live Nation has a band in the 4,500-capacity range, its only local options are the UPMC Events Center in Moon or a big undersell at the Petersen Events Center in Oakland.
That will change with the construction of a new, $64 million venue announced in cooperation with the Pittsburgh Penguins at the former Civic Arena site in Uptown. The venue is expected to go live in the first quarter of 2026, attracting more concerts to the city, while also drawing some shows away from Stage AE (an AEG venue) and the Cultural District theaters.
LN also agreed to promote at least 25 events annually for five years at the historic New Granada Theater, which is being redeveloped by the Hill Community Development Corp.
'C' is for closing
In September, within a few days, Club Cafe and Cattivo announced they would be closing their doors after decades of hosting bands.
Cattivo, in Lawrenceville, dated to the '80s, when it was the Candlelight Lounge. Frank Malta bought the grungy side-street club in 1996 and began hosting bands there in 2007, specializing in punk, indie and metal. The likes of Titus Andronicus, Lydia Lunch, The Queers, The Dickies and Subhumans passed through, as did the Skull Fest and Descendants of Crom festivals. It closed in early October.
The more refined Club Cafe, with a 125-person capacity, has been a gentler space, specializing in singer-songwriters and Americana, indie and jam bands. There has been music there since 1988, when it became Cardillo's Club Cafe. Marco Cardamone redesigned the space and reformatted the programming when he took over in 1999 and it's been in the hands of Opus One Productions since 2011.
The final show there will be New Invisible Joy on Dec. 30.
Houserockin'
Joe Grushecky, who best exemplifies the Steel City ethos of Pittsburgh rock 'n' roll, made all kinds of news in the middle of 2024.
In May, the new Bell Theater in Holmdel, N.J., opened with a premiere of "East Carson Street," a musical about working-class Pittsburgh life soundtracked by the music of the Houserockers. Broadway veterans Constantine Maroulis and Teal Wicks were in the lead roles. There is no word yet on when we might see "East Carson Street" closer to East Carson Street.
Also in May, his new label, Omnivore Recordings, released the career-spanning "Houserocker: A Joe Grushecky Anthology" in the lead-up to the July release of "Can't Outrun A Memory," the first album from Joe Grushecky and the Houserockers in seven years.
Welcome back, Donnie!
It may have slowed him down, but cancer did not stop Dahnie.
Donnie Iris got the dreaded diagnosis just after his 80th birthday bash in Moon in March 2023, sidelining him and the Cruisers for the remainder of the year.
In March 2024, it was in remission and Iris was back to celebrate his 81st birthday at the UPMC Events Center.
"I'm cancer free now," Iris told the PG. "The only thing is, you know, it took a lot out of me. It took my bladder, man. It's been a long haul."
While Donnie wasn't at full strength, performing some of the set on a barstool, he was still hitting the high notes on "Ah! Leah!"
Billy's blues
We are also lucky to have fellow Pittsburgh legend Billy Price still doing his thing.
In August, Price returned with "Person of Interest," the followup to 2019's "Dog Eat Dog." It's an album of 13 originals recorded with Grammy-winning producer/drummer Tony Braunagel with a band that included Jim Pugh and Lenny Castro, and a guest solo from Joe Bonamassa.
Lemon blend
"These people are all old," you say.
Yes, they are. On the young end of the spectrum is Merce Lemon, who earned raves for "Watch Me Drive Them Dogs Wild" (Darling Records), the followup to her 2020 breakout "Moonth."
Written during the pandemic and recorded over five days in March 2023 at Drop of Sun Studios in Asheville, N.C., with co-producers Alex Farrar and Colin Miller, the album received a 7.7 from Pitchfork and was praised by Paste for its cutting lyrics and bold composition.
Lemon is spending late December on tour with the Felice Brothers and will be with the highly acclaimed Jessica Pratt in early 2025.
More news
* In March, Fall Out Boy frontman Patrick Stump sat down at the piano at PPG Paints Arena to cover "Heart Transplant," a song from the band's former tour mates Punchline. In November, the Pittsburgh pop-punk reunited with former members Paul Menotiades and PJ Caruso at the Roxian Theatre to perform the album that song came from, "Action," for the record's 20th anniversary.
* County fairs, rib festivals, the Nick's Fat City Reunion, the Donnie Iris birthday gig … The Clarks seemed to do it all this year. Although Pittsburgh's most popular band has yet to follow 2018's "Madly in Love at the End of the World," The Clarks did treat fans to a pair of solid new singles in "Supernatural" and "No. 9."
* Jackie Evancho, who became an opera sensation at 10 years old on "America's Got Talent," appeared on "Good Morning America" to perform "Behind My Eyes," the single from "Solla," a synth-pop album recorded with Greg Camp of Smash Mouth.
* Drummer and educator Chad Taylor (of Chicago Underground, Iron&Wine and more) took over as artistic director of the University of Pittsburgh's Jazz Studies program, filling the big shoes of the late Nathan Davis, the late Geri Allen and Nicole Mitchell.
* Stop the Violence overcame a multitude of permit obstacles to hold its Juneteenth Celebration at Point State Park with Arrested Development and Beanie Sigel/Freeway, along with a seminar with hip-hop legends Master P and Dame Dash.
* There was holiday cheer from two Pittsburgh ex-pats now living in the Nashville area. Dan + Shay went big with "It's Officially Christmas: The Double Album," featuring 10 holiday classics and 11 originals all written or co-written by North Hills High School grad Dan Smyers. Gabby Barrett, the breakout star from Munhall, dropped the more sacred "Candlelight Christmas."
* 1Hood Media, led by rapper Jasiri X, was active in this election year with PowerUptheVote concerts and panels that included Fat Joe, Jeezy, Common, Redman, Method Man and Lupe Fiasco.
* Pittsburgh oldies legend Jack Hunt, aka Johnny Angel, suffered a stroke in September but returned the following month to host his Heavenly Soul Show Sunday at jukinoldies.com.
* The metal scene of the '80s, marked by the arrival of bands like Doomwatch, Dream Death and Eviction, was immortalized in the newly released documentary "In the Days of Iron and Steel: The Story of Pittsburgh Heavy Metal."
* The punk scene of the late '70s/early '80s was immortalized in "Had to Be There," a book of photos taken by Erik Bauer at Electric Banana and other venues.
* The annual tribute series that began in 2018 with Tom Petty dug into the goth/New Wave archive at the Roxian in December with Pittsburgh Plays The Cure.
* Pittsburgh punk pioneer Karl Mullen (Carsickness) returned to the city from Massachusetts with his new band Five Pound Horse.
* Riley's Pour House, one of the hubs of the Irish folk scene, was lost to fire in November.
Notable releases
* Sun King Warriors made one of the best classic-rock albums of the year with "Like a Light," its first release in six years, and the first since frontman Jim Donovan (Rusted Root) endured a near-death experience.
* WDVE morning man Randall Baumann released his first solo album, "Wanderin Fool," a funk/soul workout with help from Steeltown Horns, singers Addi Twigg and Anne Celedonia, Brian Wolfe (The Dap-Kings), Read Connolly (Zach Bryan Band) and Chris Parker.
* North Side rapper Fedd the God released "Soul Searching," his first full-length album on Wiz Khalifa's Taylor Gang label.
* Pittsburgh rapper-singer Benji., now in L.A., released the sunny pop-funk record "Love Gun 3" with backing from Spillage Village.
* The Ghost Club, fronted by the Springsteen-inspired Domenic Dunegan, got press from SPIN and Paste for the band's sophomore album "King Whatever."
* Bill Deasy, of The Gathering Field, updated his catalog with a finely crafted 10th album "Work in Progress."
* Rave Ami, the self-proclaimed "loudest band in Pittsburgh," lived up to the billing on its fourth album, "No Arc."
* Trace Remains, a band with roots in Pikadori and Allies, unleashed an epic rock sound on the debut "Leverage the Tides."
* Grand Piano, an indie band beloved for its surprising twists and turns, combined its release show at Spirit for new album "Information Superhigh" with a farewell show.
* Sun Ray, Shining Light, a post-rock band a la Explosions in the Sky, released "Lullabies for Calliope," an album recorded in 2014.
* Gene the Werewolf put the local hits all in one place on 10-song "The Best of Gene the Werewolf."
* Dichro, a local supergroup with members of Venus in Furs, Rusted Root and more, whichformed during the pandemic as a one-off virtual project to cover "I Am a Man of Constant Sorrow," issued the dark wave debut "Stained Glass."
* Post-hardcore band Rated Eye (members of Microwaves, Night Vapor, more) took listeners on a rough ride on a self-titled debut album.
* PosterChild, a band led by 19-year-old Gibson Musisko (son of Gary Musisko and Rusted Root's Jenn Wertz) made an impressive debut with an album in the vein of Radiohead and The Strokes.
* The local Get Hip Records released a 1978/1979 live album from The Puke, one of Pittsburgh's OG punk bands.
